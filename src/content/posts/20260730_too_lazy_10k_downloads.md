Earlier this week, Buzzsprout sent me one of those cheerful automated emails that platforms send when they want you to feel good about yourself. This one worked: *Too Lazy to Read the Paper* has passed **10,000 downloads**.

I never really made the podcast to have an impact. As a tenured professor, I don't have to worry about listeners or traditional success metrics. To be completely honest, it was more an excuse to ask my favorite scientists questions about their life choices and careers that might feel weird or intrusive in an everyday conversation. 

And while 10K downloads is tiny in the grand scheme of podcasts, for a scientist who does occasionally think about impact and citations of my academic outputs, I think this is a pretty wild number. Ten thousand downloads is a lot of "attention", especially considering the relatively small amount of work going into the podcast ... since it's unedited, hour-long freewheeling conversations with friends and colleagues. 

But maybe I should back up a bit and explain what the podcast is even about: the pitch of the show is that the author of a great paper in network science or data science explains that paper to me, in conversation. That has two advantages: First of all that I don't have to read it (although, secretly, between you and me, I actually do read it). Secondly and more importantly, a conversation is the perfect way to explain a paper. It's more direct and honest. Papers are sometimes formal in a way that gets in the way of the ideas. When two people just talk, the work tends to land faster. 

I ran two seasons – ten episodes over Zoom in the spring of 2021, ten more (mostly in person, mostly audio) in 2022 – and then the show took a restorative 3.5-year nap (I need to do real work sometimes too, you know) before coming back this June with [a conversation with YY Ahn about agentic coding](/blog/too-lazy-claude-code-for-science), under the temporary title *Too Lazy to Figure Out How to Use Claude Code for Science*.

And here's another important point. The 10,000 number is **only audio downloads on Buzzsprout**. 

The 13 episodes that also live on YouTube **add another 7,873 views resulting in nearly 18K views total**. (I buried the lede a bit here, but I only counted the YouTube views *after* starting the post.) 

On YouTube, Season 1 does most of the heavy lifting (88% of the views), with Marta C. González's episode 1 leading the pack at 1,404. And the eight audio-only Season 2 episodes don't appear in the YouTube numbers at all, so if anything the true reach is pleasantly undercounted.

None of this happens without the guests – twenty-three generous scientists across twenty-one episodes who sat down and explained their work to a host who had, by design, not done the reading. Thank you. And thank you for listening.
