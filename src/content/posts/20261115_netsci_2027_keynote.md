Lots of people would "be humbled", but I have to say that I'm just excited, delighted, happy, elated, and exultant to be heading to Dresden in May 2027 to give a keynote at [NetSci 2027](https://netsci2027.github.io/).

NetSci is the conference that started it all for me. It's (arguably) the main Network Science conference, and it's full of people I've known more or less since I started out in Science. My first NetSci talk was in Norwich in 2008, where I met a bunch of people I'm still friends with to this day. In 2013 I was General Chair (with Petter Holme) when the conference came to Copenhagen.[^1] To hype the conference, I wrote a slightly unhinged series of blog posts called [Nine reasons to go](/blog/netsci-2013-nine-reasons-to-go-all-of-them) (Reason 7, for example, is "the snarky/silly reason").

So being invited back as a keynote speaker feels a bit like being asked to give the toast at a family wedding: a real honor, and also mildly terrifying, because everyone in the room knows you. What will I talk about? I haven't decided yet. But I'm already beginning to freak out about it. I'll take suggestions if you have any.

The conference is hosted by the Center Synergy of Systems (SynoSys) at TU Dresden, 17–21 May 2027, with satellites and the school on 17–18 May and the main conference from 18 May. I have the same advice as I did back in 2013: [submit an abstract](https://netsci2027.github.io/calls/abstracts) on your most recent fun result (the deadline is 1 December), and come to Dresden. The very best reason to go is still all the awesome and creative people you'll meet there.

[^1]: The 2013 edition also won the Copenhagen Kongres & Event Award, which I mention purely for completeness and not at all because I'm also proud of that one.
