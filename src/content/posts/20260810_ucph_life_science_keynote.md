I'm excited to be part of the *AI in Life Science* PhD summer school at the University of Copenhagen on 10 August 2026 with a keynote on life trajectories.

The summer school, which is officially *AI and Data Science: Applications and Opportunities in Health Sciences*, runs 10–21 August and gathers early-career researchers from computer science, bioinformatics, biostatistics, epidemiology, neuroscience, and the neighbouring quantitative fields. It's a two-week, hands-on programme built around Denmark's unusually rich health-data infrastructure, and I get to be part of the first day to kick it off.

![Life trajectories rendered as a human silhouette woven from a lifetime of connected events](/assets/20260810_ucph_life_science_keynote/hero.jpg "Life trajectories: health unfolds over time.")

My talk is about life trajectories: what happens when you point the tools of network and behavioural science at large-scale *life* as well as health data and try to model how human health and disease actually unfold over time ... not as a snapshot, but as a sequence. It's the same modelling approach that drives my group's work on human lives, but here I'll focus on what life-data helps us understand about health. Looking forward to it!
